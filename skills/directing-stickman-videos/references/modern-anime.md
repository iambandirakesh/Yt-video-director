# Style 6 — Modern Anime / 现代动漫风

Cinematic modern-anime storytelling: fully drawn human characters with expressive faces and detailed costume, moving through painted, atmospheric places. Use it for character-led shorts, emotional turning points, narrative explainers and short drama. This is not a stick-figure style, not chibi comedy, not glossy 3D animation and not a photorealistic live-action look.

The style is defined from the owner's reference character sheet: a night-railway world with two leads, front, side and back turnarounds, a four-state expression set, prop plates and a location plate. Use that sheet as the production model for every new cast, not as a mandatory story or a fixed universal cast. The sheet is a design document, not a frame to reproduce; never generate its layout labels, character names or notes as visible text.

## Fixed drawing grammar

Repeat this anchor verbatim in every standalone prompt:

```text
Modern cinematic 2D anime animation with clean dark ink outlines, layered cel shading and soft filmic light. Full human characters with natural adult proportions, expressive faces, detailed eyes with small highlights, layered hair strands and clothing folds. Exactly two arms and two legs, five fingers on each hand, and one current pose per limb. Painterly atmospheric backgrounds with cinematic depth, volumetric light and drifting air. Smooth hand-drawn anime motion with weight, hair and cloth follow-through.
```

Keep face construction, eye rendering, line weight, shading model and adult proportions fixed within a story. Costume, era, weather and lighting change per story; the drawing system does not. Do not inherit Style 1's hollow-head/no-face/no-clothing contract, Style 3's crude outline grammar, Style 4's white-filled minimalist bodies or Style 5's stick limbs. Open eyes keep a drawn iris, pupil and small highlights; blinks and narrow expressions do not turn them into dots or ovals. Hands have five fingers; avoid mitten hands, duplicated fingers and fused props.

## The character sheet

Phase A delivers one compact sheet per recurring role, modeled on the attached reference:

- **Identity:** role name, age range, height and build, and two visible non-color differences from every other named role.
- **Face:** face shape, eye shape and eye color, brows, nose line, mouth line, facial hair or explicit absence, and visible age marks.
- **Hair:** silhouette, length, parting, and the strands that read in a close-up.
- **Wardrobe:** every layer from outer garment to shirt to trousers or skirt, collar and cuff detail, belt, footwear, and the fixed emblem or badge.
- **Signature prop:** shape, material, color, count and the hand that normally carries it.
- **Silhouette read:** what identifies the character at wide-shot size.
- **Expression set:** four named states that this story will actually use, for example neutral, curious, shocked and calm; or neutral, serious, shocked and worried.
- **Turnaround notes:** the details that must survive a side or back view, such as the jacket's back seam, the bag's strap side, the cap's rear band and the hair's nape line.

Freeze each approved sheet and repeat its **full description** in every prompt where that role appears. Never replace it with "the same character", a name alone or an ellipsis. The sheet is a text contract; reference images are optional when the user supplies and supports them.

### Reference cast: Night Railway

This worked example comes from the owner's reference sheet. It is a usable approved pack, not the only Style 6 cast. New stories draft a new sheet.

**Young Passenger.** Age 22–25, lean and slightly tall. Soft angular young face, warm brown eyes with small highlights, straight dark brows, short nose, calm closed mouth. Messy black hair with loose strands over the forehead. Olive-brown field jacket worn open over a white collared shirt, dark straight trousers, scuffed sneakers and dark backpack straps. Carries one worn brown leather suitcase with brass corner caps. Silhouette: slim open jacket above straight legs, suitcase hanging at one side. Expression set: neutral, curious, shocked, calm.

**Station Master.** Age 60–70, broad and upright. Weathered square face, deep-set grey eyes, thick grey brows, trimmed grey-white beard and moustache, visible age lines. Neat grey-white hair under a dark navy peaked cap with a gold circular railway badge. Dark navy railway uniform jacket, white shirt, black waistcoat, gold buttons and a small round chest emblem, dark trousers and black shoes. Carries one black railway lantern with a warm flame and one silver pocket watch on a chain. Silhouette: peaked cap, broad chest, lantern held low. Expression set: neutral, serious, shocked, worried.

**Props.** One worn brown leather suitcase with brass corner caps and stitched edges; one black metal railway lantern with a warm flame behind glass; one silver pocket watch with a chain; one navy peaked cap with a gold circular badge. Count them explicitly and keep ownership stable.

**Location plate.** A covered night railway platform: wet stone paving with lamp reflections, drifting steam, cast-iron columns, hanging amber lamps, a round station clock, dark wooden benches, and a train with warm lit windows further down the track. Deep midnight-blue exterior; amber pools of light; cool silver highlights on rails and wet stone.

**Palette.** Midnight blue, lantern amber, weathered leather brown, steam grey, moonlight silver. Name these roles in ordinary language only, never as technical color codes.

**Music.** Restrained solo piano, low sustained strings and soft ambient air, with a single low percussion hit for tension; diegetic steam, rail clack, lantern glass and pocket-watch ticks sit under the voice.

## Phase A: sheet, staging and sound

Use the existing proposal and approval workflow. In Phase A include:

- The approved character sheets for every recurring role, with expression sets and turnaround notes.
- A location plate naming the places this video uses, plus the lighting state and time of day for each.
- A prop plate with counts, owners and any visible front/back condition.
- A palette of four to six ordinary color names and what each represents.
- Planned cuts: what continues across each cut (character, prop, direction, action) and what changes (place, time, weather).
- Exact English VO with reference translation, the complete voice profile from `voice-continuity.md`, and the score direction.

For a different story, draft a fresh sheet rather than reusing the passenger and station master. Keep the drawing grammar, not the cast.

## Identity, expression and continuity

- Expressions change the brows, eyelids, gaze, mouth and posture; they never redesign the face, hair, head shape or costume. Do not reuse one worried face or one slight smile throughout a video.
- Use the approved expression names for the beats that need them. A held close-up of about one to two seconds is a valid payoff; a character does not need constant motion, but must not become an idle talking head for a whole clip.
- Act with the whole body: eye-line, head tilt, shoulder direction, weight-bearing leg, hand contact and breathing. Hands genuinely touch the suitcase handle, lantern ring, cap brim or pocket watch.
- Exactly two arms and two legs exist; a raised arm replaces its previous pose. Occlusion by steam, a coat or a foreground bench is not permission to add a replacement limb.
- Costume and hair stay identical for the whole video; do not add a scarf, change a jacket, remove the cap or restyle hair between shots. Night-to-interior lighting changes illumination, not wardrobe.
- Give every prop transfer a release, travel and receiving state. A departing character takes their suitcase; an ending state does not silently restore it to the platform.

## Anime shot language

Build the sequence like a modern anime short:

- Wide establishing shot with the character small against the place, then a medium shot or two-shot for the exchange, then a close reaction and a prop insert.
- Use slow push-ins, lateral tracking, over-the-shoulder framing, rack focus, foreground steam or lamp wipes, and a framed held final image.
- Keep the camera purposeful. Avoid constant shake, whip pans and speed-line spam; reserve impact frames and stylized speed lines for one genuine action beat, drawn as graphic effects rather than additional anatomy.
- Let weather and light carry mood: steam drift, lamp flicker, rain on stone, warm windows against cold blue air.
- Match each ending to the next opening through a look, a prop, screen direction or an explicitly named cut. A deliberate time jump or location change is allowed when Phase A names it.

## Visible text and signs

Default every generated clip to no visible words, letters, numbers, subtitles, signage copy, manga sound-effect lettering, logos or watermarks. Station signs, tickets, luggage labels and notice boards stay blank or icon-only; the clock face uses ticks and hands without readable numerals. Keep optional overlay phrases in a separate post-production note, never in the generation prompt.

## Motion and sound

Drive motion with concrete actions and readable key poses: stepping down from a carriage, lifting the lantern, checking the pocket watch, sliding the suitcase onto a bench. Use anticipation and follow-through, hair and coat movement, breath and blinks. Avoid liquid morphing, costume transformation and unrelated spectacle.

Apply the shared `voice-continuity.md` contract: freeze one complete video-level voice profile in Phase A and repeat it verbatim in every clip. A change of character age, gender, place or lighting never recasts the off-screen narrator; no lip-sync or character dialogue unless explicitly approved. Keep narration audio-only, speech-bubble free and voice-first. Keep the same score motif, tempo and instrumentation across clips; scene changes may add location acoustics to SFX but not reverb the dry narration.

## Phase B prompt assembly

Each prompt contains, in order:

1. Approved duration and ratio, 720p and 24 FPS targets, synchronized audio.
2. Full drawing-style anchor, palette roles and the clip's location plate with its lighting state.
3. Full character-sheet descriptions for every visible recurring role, plus the expression state used in this clip, and the prop designs and counts.
4. Opening layout, inherited state, hand and owner of each prop, and screen direction.
5. `[0–3s]`, `[3–7s]`, `[7–10s]` beats with camera work and physical action.
6. Exact approved audio-only VO in quotation marks.
7. The complete frozen voice block from `voice-continuity.md`.
8. Score, timed SFX and voice-first mix.
9. Ending state inherited by the next clip.
10. Compatible negative constraints.

```text
NO VISIBLE TEXT, LETTERS, NUMBERS, SUBTITLES, SIGNAGE COPY, MANGA SOUND-EFFECT LETTERING, LOGOS OR WATERMARKS, NO SPEECH BUBBLES, NO 3D CGI OR PHOTOREALISTIC LIVE ACTION, NO CHIBI OR SUPER-DEFORMED PROPORTIONS, NO STICK FIGURES, NO EXTRA LIMBS OR FINGERS, NO FUSED OR DUPLICATE HEADS, NO COSTUME CHANGES, NO EXTRA CHARACTERS.
```

Keep negatives compatible with the approved cast: do not ban drawn anime faces, hair, hands or clothing. Do not add a style-specific character-count cap; remove conflicting or redundant wording rather than dropping identity details. A deliberate reaction pause is not an idle-character failure, and a narrative short may end on an image or a line instead of a discussion question.

## Review checklist

- The source meaning is preserved, and any invented scene is not presented as fact.
- Every recurring role has a complete sheet, with two visible non-color differences and an approved expression set.
- The full sheet, expression state, prop counts and location plate repeat in every relevant prompt.
- Two arms, two legs, five fingers per hand, stable face and hair, unchanged costume.
- Anime drawing grammar holds: ink outlines, cel shading, painted depth; no stick limbs, chibi bodies or 3D rendering.
- Prop ownership, hand contact and ending state match the action; departed props stay departed.
- Light and weather change mood without changing wardrobe or character identity.
- No readable text, signage copy, manga lettering, captions or interface copy was generated.
- The complete voice block is identical across clips, separate from score and scene emotion.
- Adjacent clips connect through a named action, prop, direction or approved cut.
